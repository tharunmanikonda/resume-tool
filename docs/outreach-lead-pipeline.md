# Outreach Lead Pipeline

Use this document as the manual workflow for turning daily small-company lead
discovery into calls. Later, this can become a database table and app view.

## Goal

Find US-based small or mid-sized companies where direct email or direct-message
outreach to a founder, CTO, engineering leader, or technical operator could
lead to a conversation, interview, contract, or warm referral.

The best targets are companies where Tharun's software engineering background
and current resume-tool work create a specific pitch:

- AI agents
- Workflow automation
- Browser automation
- Document, email, PDF, or form processing
- ATS, recruiting, or job workflow tooling
- MCP or tool integrations
- LLM evaluation and review loops
- Technical operations automation
- B2B SaaS with messy customer workflows
- Customer-facing implementation, solutions, or technical GTM

## Daily Flow

1. Run daily lead discovery.
2. Review the 5-10 returned companies.
3. Prioritize the list so the strongest companies are contacted first.
4. Contact every qualified company from the daily list.
5. Spend extra research time on the highest-scoring companies, but do not skip
   lower-scoring companies unless they are clearly not a fit.
6. Identify 1-3 people to contact at each company.
7. Find public LinkedIn and X/Twitter profiles for the company and each target
   person when available.
8. Draft a short personalized message for each contact.
9. Send the messages.
10. Record status and follow-up date.

Job postings are useful only as context. The primary motion is not to apply
through job boards. The primary motion is to find the right person and email or
message them directly.

### Fit

How closely the company maps to Tharun's strongest pitch.

- 5: Direct match to AI agents, workflow automation, browser automation, document processing, MCP/tooling, or technical implementation.
- 3: General software engineering or AI fit.
- 1: Weak or generic fit.

### Timing

Whether the company has a reason to need people now.

- 5: Recent funding, launch, customer traction, new roles, founder hiring post, or visible growth.
- 3: Active company with some signs of movement.
- 1: No clear timing signal.

### Reachability

How easy it is to reach a decision-maker.

- 5: Founder/CTO name plus public email or clear direct route.
- 3: Founder/CTO profile found, but only LinkedIn, X, contact form, or company
  inbox route.
- 1: Only generic ATS route or unclear contacts.

### Role Match

How well the visible role or likely need fits.

- 5: Founding Engineer, AI Engineer, Forward Deployed Engineer, Solutions Engineer, Technical Ops, GTM Engineer, Implementation Engineer, or similar.
- 3: General engineering role.
- 1: No obvious technical or hybrid need.

### Company Size

How well the company fits the desired size range.

- 5: 10-50 employees.
- 4: 1-10 or 50-100 employees.
- 2: 100-250 employees.
- 1: Too large or unclear.

## Outreach Rule

This is a high-volume, high-fit outreach motion. Do not limit outreach to only
the top 3 companies. The score determines order and effort level, not whether a
company gets contacted.

- Contact every qualified company found in the daily lead run.
- Prefer founder, CEO, CTO, head of engineering, founder-led hiring inbox, or a
  public personal/company email over any job-board application.
- Capture LinkedIn and X/Twitter profiles for both the company and target
  contacts whenever they are publicly available.
- Treat generic inboxes such as `sales@`, `info@`, `support@`, and `inquiry@`
  as low-value fallback routes. Do not count them as strong reachability unless
  the company explicitly says hiring or founders read that inbox.
- For high-score companies, send more personalized messages and contact multiple
  relevant people.
- For medium-score companies, send a concise but still specific message.
- Skip only companies that are clearly too large, outside the US, unrelated to
  technical work, or impossible to contact through a reasonable public route.

## Contact Discovery Method

Use company social activity to find the real people behind the company.

### LinkedIn

1. Open the company LinkedIn page.
2. Check recent company posts for founders, hiring managers, engineers, and
   people commenting with insider context.
3. Check employees listed on the company page for CEO, founder, CTO, head of
   engineering, product, solutions, GTM, or operations roles.
4. Check who reposted or commented on hiring posts. Founders often repost roles
   from personal accounts.
5. Save the company LinkedIn URL and each useful contact LinkedIn URL.

### X / Twitter

1. Open the company X profile when available.
2. Check recent launch, hiring, funding, and product update posts.
3. Check replies, reposts, tagged people, and bios for founders or builders.
4. Search the company name plus `founder`, `CTO`, `hiring`, or the product name.
5. Save the company X URL and each useful contact X URL.

### Contact Priority

Prefer contacts in this order:

1. Founder / CEO with public email or active LinkedIn/X profile.
2. CTO / technical co-founder.
3. Head of engineering / founding engineer.
4. Product, solutions, implementation, technical ops, or GTM leader.
5. Founder-led hiring inbox, personal work email, or explicit "email me" route.
6. Generic company inbox only if it is the only reasonable route and the company
   is a very strong fit.
7. Generic careers/apply route only as backup context.

### Weak Contact Routes

These routes should not be treated as good outreach routes by themselves:

- `sales@...`
- `info@...`
- `support@...`
- `contact@...`
- `inquiry@...`
- generic website forms
- generic job-board apply links

Use them only as backup when there is also a named founder/CTO LinkedIn or X
profile, or when the company explicitly asks candidates to email that address.

Focus on technical fit and systems built.

Template:

> I saw [company] is working on [specific technical area]. I have been building
> production-style AI workflows across Flask, React, browser extensions,
> document/PDF generation, ATS/form automation, and MCP-style tooling. I think I
> could be useful on [specific need]. Open to a quick chat?

### Solutions / GTM / Operations Leader

Focus on customer workflows and implementation.

Template:

> I noticed [company] is helping customers with [specific workflow]. My recent
> work sits between engineering and operations: AI workflow tooling, browser
> automation, structured document generation, and application-process
> automation. I would love to see whether I can help with implementation,
> customer workflows, or technical operations.

## Weekly Review

At the end of each week, review:

- Which company types replied?
- Which contact type replied: founder, CTO, engineering leader, ops, GTM?
- Which pitch worked best?
- Which source produced the best leads?
- Which roles gave the most traction?
- Which leads need follow-up?
